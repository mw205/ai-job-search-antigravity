Most developers and freelancers treat the job search as a spray-and-pray game.

The result? Low response rates, generic cover letters that sound like AI templates, and hours wasted formatting LaTeX and checking ATS margins.

A few months ago, Mads Lorentzen showed how an engineer can approach this like a software system by open-sourcing `ai-job-search` for Claude Code.

Today, I’m open-sourcing an enhanced edition engineered to work seamlessly out-of-the-box across **Google Antigravity, OpenCode, and Claude Code**:

🚀 **AI Job Search (Antigravity & Multi-Runtime Edition)**
A local, multi-agent career operating system that turns your AI assistant into your full-time job discovery and application partner.

Whether you run **Google Antigravity**, **OpenCode**, or **Claude Code**, you can open this repository and use it **as it is** — no duplicate configuration, no vendor lock-in.

Here’s what it does locally on your machine:

1️⃣ **Automated Multi-Portal Discovery**
Built-in CLI scrapers search LinkedIn, FreeHire, and tech job boards for remote, freelance, and contract positions matching your exact tech stack.

2️⃣ **Prompt-Injection Defense**
Job postings are untrusted web data. An automated safety gate filters hidden HTML, tracking tokens, and malicious prompts before the AI ever evaluates the text.

3️⃣ **Two-Agent Drafter-Reviewer Architecture**
The primary agent drafts your tailored application, and an adversarial "Hiring Manager" subagent independently critiques it against real company research and your candidate truth base.

4️⃣ **Strict LaTeX Compilation & ATS Parseability Audit**
No broken formatting or guesswork:
• Your CV compiles to **exactly 2 pages** with `lualatex`.
• Your cover letter compiles to **exactly 1 page** with `xelatex`.
• A mechanical Python verifier audits the text layer to ensure Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) read every contact detail and keyword cleanly.

5️⃣ **100% Privacy-Preserving**
Runs completely on your own machine. Your contact details, salary targets, and application history stay in your private repository.
